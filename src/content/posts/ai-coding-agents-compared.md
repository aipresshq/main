## What happened

Coding agents now span a wide range of working styles. Some stay close to an editor and propose small changes; others can inspect a repository, run commands, and carry a task across several files. Comparing them requires more than counting features.

## What to compare

Start with control. A useful agent should make its plan legible, show the files it changes, and leave a clear path for review. Teams working in sensitive repositories may prefer frequent approval points, while a well-contained maintenance task may benefit from more autonomy.

Context handling matters just as much. Look at how reliably an agent finds project instructions, follows established patterns, and keeps related changes together. A large context allowance is less valuable if the agent overlooks local conventions or loses track of the original request.

The surrounding workflow can decide the result. Check support for your editor, terminal, issue tracker, and code-review process. Also consider how clearly the product reports usage, permissions, and failures.

## A practical choice

Give each candidate the same representative task in a non-critical repository. Compare the quality of its reasoning, the size and focus of its changes, and how much cleanup a reviewer must do. Prefer the agent that produces understandable, reversible work and fits the way your team already ships software.
